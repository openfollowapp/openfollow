# Paths

Covers `paths/`, the pathfinder lock and the path editor. The rules that apply everywhere are in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md).

Experimental and config-only (the `paths` feature): not in the Features table, on only through `[ui] shown_features` in `config.toml`. A path is a list of PSN-absolute X/Y/Z points a marker can be locked onto and walked along.

- **`paths/geometry.py`**: `build_curve` turns a path into a `PathCurve`, straight between its points with each interior corner (every corner when closed) rounded by an arc of that point's radius. An arc takes at most half of either neighbouring segment, so a radius too big for the corner shrinks. Up to a 120° turn the arc keeps the full radius; past it the arc tightens the sharper the turn (it meets each segment `3 × radius / tan(turn / 2)` from the point), closing on the point as the turn reaches straight back, so an out-and-back path reaches its far point and a turn close to straight back draws nearly the same curve as one exactly straight back, which is sharp at the point. The curve is a dense polyline whose chords stay within 1 mm of the arcs, with no step shorter than a micrometre (a path left with fewer than two distinct samples is no curve, and a closed one with fewer than three is open), addressed by arc length `s`: `point`, `tangent`, `directions` (arriving and leaving, which differ on a sharp corner, so a walk can leave one by either segment), `nearest`, `advance` (an open curve reports which end stopped it), `to_vertex` (how far the next vertex is either way), `offset` (the shorter way round a loop). Z follows linearly along the straight parts. Each arc lies in the plane of its two segments, so a bend in height is rounded like a turn in plan and the height changes smoothly through it, not linearly.
- **`paths/network.py`**: `PathNetwork` holds the active paths' curves and their junctions. Two paths meet where they cross in plan at the same height (`JUNCTION_HEIGHT_M`, 0.2 m; a crossing counts up to `_ON_SEGMENT` of a segment past its ends, so one through a sharp corner point is not lost to rounding), and a path whose end lies within `JUNCTION_REACH_M` (0.2 m) of another joins it there. An arc passes its point by, so before junctions are found `extend_to_corners` carries on an open end that lies within reach of another path's corner point (`PathCurve.corners`: the inner points of an open path, every point of a closed one) and does not lie on another curve's side at its height (running on would leave that curve): the end runs on straight, its height changing linearly, to where that path's curve passes nearest the point (the arc's middle, at the same height), and joins there by touch, so a marker crosses over without a jump. The old end becomes a sharp inner point. Another path's end touching ours does not stop it, as it still touches our curve afterwards (so several spurs ending on one corner point all run on and meet at the arc), unless that end runs on to a different corner, which would part the two: then both stay. `PathNetwork.curves` are the carried-on curves, which is what is walked, projected onto and drawn. Paths that only turn at the same point join only where their curves meet. A path crossing itself, or a loop coming close without crossing, makes no junction. `find_junctions` compares two paths segment by segment only where the bounding boxes of runs of `_BOX_SEGMENTS` segments come within reach of each other, `_BOX_PAIRS_PER_PASS` pairs of boxes at a time so a dense pair takes bounded memory, and looks for a repeat junction only among those already kept within a few centimetres of it. A network holds at most `MAX_JUNCTIONS` (1024) junctions: the search takes pairs of paths in order and stops once it finds one more, keeping the first 1024 and logging one WARNING that the rest are left out, so a tangle of paths crossing each other everywhere still builds in bounded time. `walk` moves a locked marker by the part of the push that points along its path, in legs that each end at the next vertex, so past a corner the marker moves at the rate of the segment it is on and stops on a corner where no way on has a share of the push (at most `_MAX_STEPS` legs, `_MAX_LEGS` of them reaching a junction); at a junction it takes the direction closest to the push, leaving its own path only when the other is more than `_SWITCH_MARGIN_RAD` (15°) closer; of two turns as close as each other the nearer junction's wins, and a way off another path's end that releases is never a turn (only the marker's own releasing end lets it go). A marker within the switch area (along its path either side of a junction: `SWITCH_AREA_M`, 0.5 m, or the push's speed times `SWITCH_LOOKAHEAD_S`, 0.5 s, whichever is more) can steer into the other path; it first runs along its own path to the junction at the push's speed, then turns, so a switch never jumps. A moving marker stops each step where the next switch area starts, and inside one at its junction, and decides again there; a junction within `_EPS` of an area's start counts as inside it, so no leg is a rounding error the curve cannot advance by. Pushing past an open end stops there, or reports `released` when that end releases. `switch_arrows` lists, for a marker inside a switch area, a short stretch (`SWITCH_ARROW_FROM_M` to `SWITCH_ARROW_TO_M` along the curve) of every way onto another path at that junction, as (tail, tip), and none along the marker's own path (drawn white over it, it would not show); each other path and direction shows once, at the nearest junction (an end touching a path it also crosses makes two a few centimetres apart), and an end closer than the stretch shows none. `nearest` projects a position onto the nearest place over every path or among some, and `connected` names the paths reachable from one through junctions. `follow` is where a position puts a locked marker: the nearest point of its own path, or of another path that meets it at a junction, only when the position lies within `SWITCH_AREA_M` (the 0.5 m floor, never the area widened by speed) plus one sample's step (`_POSITION_STEP_M`) of that junction and the other path's nearest point lies within the same distance of it, measured along that path; the nearest of those wins, a tie stays put. A bridge crossing overhead, a path met only far away, or one passing back over the junction higher up is never taken, and a position far along another branch lands on the marker's own path. A move across several junctions happens one per sample. Junction points are cached per network (`_junction_points`).
- **Names** are what OSC addresses: `[A-Za-z0-9._-]`, at most 32, unique ignoring case, and never dots alone, which a URL resolves away. `PathsConfig.__post_init__` reduces a bad name and renames a clash (`-2`, `-3`) rather than dropping geometry; caps are `MAX_PATHS` (32) and `MAX_PATH_POINTS` (100).
- **Active is per station.** `PathsConfig.replace_paths` takes another source's paths but keeps this station's Active flag per name (a new name arrives inactive); every route that writes paths from outside uses it: the `paths` section of the JSON API and section broadcast, import and Broadcast All (which carry paths, unlike zones), and the `paths` template type. `show_paths` is this station's view: the JSON API, a section broadcast, Broadcast All and a `paths` template leave it alone (`_DEVICE_LOCAL_FIELDS_BY_SECTION`), and only an imported settings file restores it. A `paths` value that is not a list leaves the paths alone.
- **`paths/engine.py`**: `PathEngine` (`app._paths`, rebuilt by `reload` whenever `[paths]` changes or the `paths` feature flips; with it off nothing is active, nobody stays locked and nothing records) holds each locked marker's place, in memory only. A Pathfinder press (`press`) pulls an unlocked marker onto the nearest active path over `slide_time`: its input keeps moving a free position, and what is written is eased from that position to the nearest point of the path, so the marker carries on and lands where its movement took it; a second press, Reset, deactivating the path or deleting it releases. An edit to the path puts the marker back on the edited curve, written by the next `tick`. **Every movement source asks the engine before writing a locked marker and writes what it answers**, so no output ever carries a position off the path: relative input through `InputManager._move` (`move`, Z inputs ignored, height is the path's; a push past an end that releases lets the marker go there), OSC positions and the Fully Automatic pin through `project` (along the path, onto another only at a junction, `follow`; during the pull a per-axis OSC message takes the axes it leaves out from the free position, `free_position`; past an end they stop there, even at one that releases, so noise near the end of an external source never drops the lock). AI Assisted constrains the operator's anchor, which is what input drives, and writes its output where `constrain` answers: where `project` would put it, without moving the lock the anchor holds; with no video resolution it constrains a locked output where it stands. When assist stops (Tracking off, or Fully Automatic), the next frame's `update_paths` drops the anchor before anything writes a marker (`hand_over_anchors`) and hands its lock to the output with `take_over`, so input carries on from where the output stood: landed, at the reachable point nearest it, easing from its height; mid-pull, pulled in afresh from it. The 2D mouse never moves a marker along a path: a grab releases a locked marker where it stands (mid-pull included), a lock taken while the mouse holds a marker ends the grab before the mouse writes again, and the wheel leaves a locked marker's height alone. `InputManager.update_paths` runs the engine once a frame, called by `animate` after the frame's discrete key presses (Reset, Pathfinder) and before `process_input`, so an open menu or a station controlling no marker never stops it: it hands over the locks of anchors assist no longer drives, releases locks of markers no longer controlled, applies the OSC path commands, then writes what `tick` moved.
- **`runtime/paths_runtime.py`** is the frame-loop glue: Pathfinder presses (`InputManager.pathfinder` from the keyboard's selected marker, per pad as `GamepadUpdate.pathfinder_presses`, per puck), recording (a press while `engine.recording` appends the steered marker's X/Y/Z to the path as the running config and the file each have it, so a path made on the web moments ago records too), Toggle paths (`show_paths`, saved), OSC commands, and the blue `path_recording` status row. Each save writes only its change into the config file as it is on disk (`save_config_change`, under `config_write_lock`) and leaves the file unmarked as loaded, so a web save the reload watcher has not applied yet survives and is still applied; the watcher's own pass is a no-op for paths that match the running config. The bindings (`btn_pathfinder`, `btn_toggle_paths`, `key_pathfinder`, `key_toggle_paths`, `mouse3d.btn_pathfinder` / `btn_toggle_paths`) start unbound, sit last in their form groups, and their form rows carry `experimental-feature`; the help overlay names them only while paths run.
- **OSC** (`osc/paths.py`, subscribed while the OSC listener is up): `/path/<name>/active <on>` switches a path (saved), `/marker/<id>/path <name>` locks a controlled marker onto an active path (`lock_onto`; a marker already on that path, or pulled onto it, is left as it is, so a desk resending the lock never restarts the pull), no name releases. Queued (at most `MAX_PENDING`, the oldest dropped) and applied on the frame loop in order; a frame's switches reach the file and the engine once, net (one save, one rebuild, nothing for a path switched back), and only a lock onto a path switched earlier in the same frame rebuilds the engine first. Ignored while experimental features are off.
- **Operator Screen**: active paths (and the one recording) are drawn by `overlay_draw_paths` after zones, grey and semi-transparent, opaque white while a marker is on one (`COLOR_PATH` / `COLOR_PATH_LOCKED`), with a dot on an open path's end that holds and an arrowhead pointing out of one that releases; the drawn line is the curve with straight runs split every 0.25 m so the lens bows it. `PathCache` keeps every path, grey, as one picture and the paths with a marker on them, white, as a second picture over it: a marker crossing a junction redraws only the second. Both are let go while no path is drawn (the second while no marker is on a path), and each is drawn again when a curve, an end, the camera, the lens, the frame size or the device scale changes; a curve is the engine's own list until the paths reload, so the key compares by identity. `PathEngine.switch_arrows` asks the network for each landed lock at the speed of that lock's push this frame (`tick` clears it before the frame's input, so a frame without a push uses the 0.5 m floor), and each is drawn as a white arrow every frame, outside `PathCache` (`OverlayState.path_arrows`).
- **Web UI** (Markers & Zones tab, both sections `data-feature="paths"`): the Paths form (`partials/paths.tpl`, `/section/paths`: show_paths, slide_time) and the Path Editor (`partials/path_editor.tpl`, outside the form so a Save swaps only the form), one OSC-Transmitter-style row per path (Basics: Active, Name, Shape; Points: X / Y / Z and an Option column, a corner's radius or an open end's Hold / Release, reordered by the shared ⋮⋮ drag-reorder in `base.tpl`, which hands a list without `data-reorder-url` a `rows-reordered` event; Record, Delete) above the camera picture. The editor talks JSON to `/api/paths` (list, create; `PUT /<name>` for name, options and the whole point list; `/<name>/active`, `/<name>/record`, `DELETE`), `/api/paths/marker/<id>` (a marker's broadcast X/Y/Z for "Point from"), `/api/paths/view` (every path, its points and ends seen by the **saved** camera and lens in a `w` x `h` picture, null where the camera cannot see) and the shared `/api/scene/unproject` (a picture point onto the plane at a height, 422 above the horizon). The snapshot, SVG overlay and point handles are the shared `static/js/picture-view.js`. Recording and locks come from the engine through the web server's `path_state_provider` / `path_recorder`; a rename is staged on the engine (`path_renamer` → `PathEngine.stage_rename`, under its own lock) before the file is saved, keyed by the name the path had at the last reload so names swapped or reused before the reload still follow their paths, and the first reload whose paths hold the new name moves the path's locks and recording onto it; `PathEngine.web_state` names them by the staged renames meanwhile; recording reads the paths from the file, which the web UI may have written a moment before the running config reloads. Edits apply in the page at once and save as they are made, one request at a time in order, each `PUT` carrying only the field it changed from the page's latest copy of the path; a recording path's points are not editable there. Every control's id names its path, so a redraw puts focus back in the same path. There is no Save per row.
- **Turning the `paths` feature off writes nothing:** every path keeps its Active flag, and the engine, disabled, lets every marker go.
